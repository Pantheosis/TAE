## Heading and citation conventions (the OCR caveat)

Dykes numbers TBN by Book, chapter and bracketed section (`[Chapter I.4: …]`, `[4.3: The years of
the kadukhudhāh]`) and Abū Bakr by Book, topic and chapter (`Chapter II.5.14`). **There are no
sentence numbers in this volume** — neither author is sentence-numbered in PN II — so citations
here are `TBN I.4.3`, `Abū Bakr II.5.14`, with the PDF page marker `*[PN II pdf p. N]*` after each.
Footnotes run continuously across the whole volume (1–1457), so a footnote number is unambiguous.

**The OCR is readable and untrustworthy in the same breath.** Quote sparingly; every quotation
below is marked **(OCR, unverified)** and must be re-read against the marker OCR or the page before
it goes near a page. The recurrent defects, so that nobody rediscovers them:

- `Satum` for Saturn; `hildj`/`hilQj`/`hlldj` for *hīlāj*; `kadukfjudhdh`/`kaduktudhah` for
  *kadukhudhāh*; `mubta~`/`mubtat^`/`muhta~` for *mubtazz*; `jdrbakfjtSr` for *jārbakhtār*;
  `dusturiyyah` survives.
- Footnote numbers run into the preceding word as superscript digits (`Mars2114`,
  `nourishment,S3'`) or stand as `*NN`/`,NN`; a footnote's own text is usually worse than the body.
- Running heads and printed folios are garbled (`PERSIAN NATTVmBS II`, `xvti`), which is why the
  PDF index is the citable page here.
- Chapter headings are often maimed: `Chapter ILL2`, `Chapter 1133`, `Chapter 157` (= II.5.4),
  `Chapter n.4.4`; the TOC's PDF pp. 7–8 lost their chapter numbers altogether. Locate chapters by
  title, not number.
- **Every figure is lost.** Figures 1–7 and the Appendix C example chart survive as scattered
  glyphs (`H n`, `T "5`, `0 15°`). Dykes' footnotes describe what each showed and that is all that
  is recoverable.
- Ordinal/cardinal confusion in the worked examples is **printed**, not OCR: Dykes says so at
  fn 190, 640.
- The Introduction's section numbering runs §1 (its heading is OCR'd as `//; New insists:
  prosperity, the nrubtat*`), §2, then **§4**, §5. Whether §3 was lost to the OCR or is a
  printer's skip cannot be told from this file; check the page.

Two printed oddities to know before citing: Abū Bakr has **two chapters numbered II.7.37**
(the body part, then the time, of an illness) — the TOC prints it so; and **II.7.0 (the preface to
illness) is entirely Dykes' bracketed paraphrase of Jag.**, absent from the 1540 print (fn 1004).

## 0. What these two books are, and how Sahl uses them

Two Latin natal works of the 'Umar lineage, both translated by John of Spain's school and both
absorbed into Bonatti, Abū 'Alī's *JN* and, through them, Lilly.

**TBN** (c. 800; 'Umar died c. 815) is short — 78 printed pages — and Dykes has **rearranged it**
(Intro §4, PDF pp. 19–21): paragraphs moved from Book III to Book I, from Book II to I.4.3–4,
interpolations from other works removed to Appendix A, section titles and the bracketed `[1]…[9]`
significator lists supplied. Cite it as Dykes numbers it and know that the Hand/Schmidt 1997
numbering does not correspond. Book I is birth and longevity, Book II annual methods, Book III
topics in a fixed template: a bracketed list of places, "the *mubtazz* over these places, whether it
were one or two", and its concord with the *mubtazz* of the Ascendant "from the fourteen ways" —
a horary structure applied to nativities (Intro §2, PDF p. 18).

**Abū Bakr** (b. c. 821 on Dykes' argument, PDF p. 17) is the third part of a four-part work; this
is its first English translation, from the 1540 Petreius print collated against Kraków MS BJ 793
(*Jag.*), which Dykes received six weeks before publication and which "usually speaks of 'signs'"
where 1540 says "houses" (PDF p. 23). Book I is overview, gestation, rearing and longevity; Book II
is topics, in the same 'Umar template but with hundreds of specific configurations, most of unknown
source (PDF p. 24). It is the volume's bulk, 255 printed pages.

**How Sahl uses them.** Sahl's *On Nativities* never names either author. Dykes' footnotes on Sahl
(§6 below) cite TBN eleven times, all in the house-master material (Sahl 1.20–1.22 ↔ TBN I.4.3–4)
and in cross-references to Abū Bakr for a garbled sentence (2.19, 4), a paternity rule (4.1) and
the Lot of Spirit (9.5, 73–91). The relationship is one of **shared Persian sources** (Dorotheus in
'Umar's own Pahlavi-to-Arabic translation; al-Andarzaghar behind Māshā'allāh's BA), not of Sahl
reading 'Umar. The one direct textual bridge Dykes claims is that Sahl's *On Questions* rests on a
lost Māshā'allāh, because 'Umar's Appendix A.1 excerpt attributes to Māshā'allāh a passage Sahl has
at *Quest.* §9.3 (fn 1434).

**Why the app cares.** Three things: (i) the **weighted victor** — Dykes' Introduction argues at
length (PDF pp. 15–17) that the 5-4-3-2-1 *mubtazz* was invented by al-'Anbas al-Ṣaymarī between
815 and 844, on the evidence of Abū Bakr II.5.14; the app's `VICTOR_WEIGHTS` names 'Umar for a
scheme, and §7.1 checks the numbers; (ii) the **sect delineations** of the course's Lesson 10,
which cite Abū Bakr for Mars, found at II.1.0 (§7.2); (iii) the **house-master's years**, where TBN
I.4.3–4 is the parallel text Dykes reads Sahl 1.20–1.21 against, including the two sentences the app
prints and does not apply (§7.4).

**The weighted *mubtazz*, as Dykes states it (PDF pp. 15–16):** "the domicile Lord of a place
received 5, the exalted Lord (if there was one) received 4, the primary triplicity Lord 3, the bound
Lord 2, and the face or decan Lord 1" **(OCR, unverified)**. "It is not found in Sahl or
Māshā'allāh or Abū 'Alī's JN. 'Umar himself seems to follow Ptolemy" (one point per dignity, *Tet.*
III.5). The chain: Abū Bakr II.5.9 (al-'Anbas mentored him, 844), II.5.10 (his father watched 'Umar
work), II.5.14 (al-'Anbas "had found the *mubtazz* according to what 'Umar said, by giving 5 …").
Dykes' conclusion, in his own words: the weighted victor "is misguided and artificial" (PDF p. 17)
— an editorial verdict, not doctrine, and the same trap PN IV's Intro §3 sets.

**Fn 16 (PDF p. 20):** Hand and Schmidt (1997) "believed that 'Umar used a compound weighted
*mubtazz*", misled by later texts. **Fn 48 (PDF p. 32, on TBN I.3.2):** "'Umar's *mubtazz* might
not even have been determined by a counting mechanism at all — or at least definitely not from one
weighted by dignity" **(OCR, unverified)**.

**Fn 10 (PDF p. 16):** Jag. attributes the weighting not to al-'Anbas but to an "Asemc-zael",
probably al-Ḥasan bin Sahl (782–851); Dykes prefers 1540's reading. So even the attribution to
al-'Anbas rests on one of two witnesses.

## 2. TBN Book I (PDF pp. 27–48; printed 1–22) — birth and longevity

- **I.1** — the seven planetary months of gestation, Chaldean order from Saturn; the 8th month
  returns to Saturn "and he will die", the 9th to Jupiter. Moved here from the end of Book III.
- **I.2** — rectifying an unknown Ascendant from the prenatal syzygy: the planet with most
  dignities *and aspecting* the syzygy degree is the "model" (Ar. *namūdār*); if it is nearer the
  horizon, set the Ascendant to its degree; if nearer the meridian, the MC. Fn 33 aspect outranks
  dignity count — the Ptolemaic count with an aspect tie-break.
- **I.3** — **the four determinations of births**: not nourished and no life; taste but not
  nourished; nourished but not to old age; nourished to old age. Each has its list of places
  (Ascendant, angles, luminaries and their lords, the three triplicity lords of the Ascendant, the
  Lot of Fortune and lord, the prenatal syzygy and lord) and "the *mubtazz* over these places,
  whether it were one or two or three". I.3.2 times death from the Ascendant "a month to every
  sign" then "a year to every 30°" (fn 41: Māshā'allāh gives a month per degree). I.3.3: for the
  third kind, defer judgment "until the Ascendant runs through one orb — this is twelve years", then
  direct a year per degree by the ascensions of the clime.
- **I.4.1 — the *hīlāj*.** Sun by day in the Ascendant, 11th or MC in any sign; in the 8th or 9th
  only in a masculine sign; **only if the lord of its domicile, bound, exaltation or triplicity
  aspects it** — the rule that applies to all five candidates. Then the Moon in an angle or
  succedent in a feminine sign; then **the Lot of Fortune if preventional, the Ascendant if
  conjunctional**; then the other of those two; then the syzygy degree. Ptolemy's and Dorotheus'
  dissents on the 8th/9th; fn 58 notes 'Umar (and Abū Bakr after him) wrongly lets Ptolemy allow a
  *hīlāj* below the earth. Fn 55: the "wife/husband" gloss on the two words is the medieval
  translator's, not 'Umar's.
- **I.4.2 — the *kadukhudhāh*.** "Of them, you will begin from the Lord of the bound: which if it
  projected its own rays to that bound … it will be the *kadukhudhāh*" **(OCR, unverified)**;
  otherwise the domicile, exaltation or triplicity lord that aspects; more dignities wins; then
  stronger place; then nearer the angle "or to the Sun, if it were in its arising". A "partner": a
  planet in the Ascendant or MC with a dignity in the *hīlāj*'s place. Dorotheus' stations rule
  (fn 69). Ptolemy: aspect itself counted as a dignity (fn 71: a misreading of *Tet.* III.11).
  Fn 74 prints the two stray Book I sentences on Dorotheus vs Ptolemy (aspect vs. count).
- **I.4.3 — the years.** Two blocks. First: oriental, angular (ASC/MC), in *ḥayyiz*, in its
  dignity → greater; oriental, dignified, succedent → middle; cadent → lesser; "with fall,
  retrogradation, and peregrination, or descension" → hours by the lesser years. Then adding and
  subtracting: a fortune aspecting adds its lesser years (months if retrograde or burned); an
  infortune by square/opposition/conjunction subtracts its lesser years; Mercury goes with whichever
  side he is on. Second block (moved from Book II, fn 79): in domicile/exaltation/triplicity in the
  MC, ASC or 11th by day, 4th and 5th by night → greater; peregrine and occidental there → lesser;
  **"peregrination and setting and retrogradation or burning up does not harm the higher planets so
  much as it does the inferior planets. But if the higher planets were impeded by a serious
  impediment, it signifies months or days according to the number of its own lesser years"
  (OCR, unverified)**; outside its dignities in angles/succedents → its years "whether it were
  peregrine or whatever its condition was — unless it were burned up"; cadent → lesser; burned →
  days or hours. Fn 80: 'Umar has left out the sect status of the *kadukhudhāh*. **This is the
  parallel to Sahl 1.20, 10–15 (Sahl fns 151, 154, 156).**
- **I.4.4 — adding and subtracting** (fn 84: also moved from Book II). Fortunes add lesser years if
  not under the rays; **"if the *kadukhudhāh* were burned up, and [therefore] it signified nothing,
  and Venus and Jupiter (or one of them) were in the Ascendant or in the Midheaven … hope of life
  … according to the lesser years of Venus or Jupiter" (OCR, unverified)** — the condition Sahl
  1.21, 14 lacks (Sahl fn 170); fortunes from square/opposition "do not cut off anything" and add if
  not retrograde/burned; infortunes from square/opposition/one sign subtract "whether they received
  it or not"; by trine/sextile only under besiegement (fn 87). **The nodes: Tail with a malefic
  *kadukhudhāh* within 12° subtracts one-fourth; Head with a benefic *kadukhudhāh* within 12° adds
  one-fourth.** Retrogradation "will take away one step from the rank [of the type of years]",
  burning another (fn 89: 'Umar has not yet used the middle years). Under the rays the
  *kadukhudhāh* "will be practically blind" to the fortunes unless in the Sun's degree. The Sun
  subtracts by conjunction/opposition/square, adds by trine/sextile; with reception, months or days.
  A malefic in angles/succedents in its exaltation, bound or triplicity gives greater years "because
  it is of a good substance".
- **I.4.5** — direct the *hīlāj* to the infortunes' bodies and rays "through the degrees of the
  ascension of his region, a year to every degree"; kills when the years equal the *kadukhudhāh*'s
  and no fortune's ray falls in that bound; Dorotheus' latitude rule (a malefic off the *hīlāj*'s
  path in latitude endangers, does not kill); "you should not direct … unless you use the latitude
  of the planets and fixed stars".
- **I.4.6 — Figure 1**, the Sun at 15 Capricorn on the MC, Saturn's square at 22: 14° vs 20° 29′ of
  ascensions, 6° 30′ = 6½ years to the square. Fn 104: the diagram is not in the text; Dykes and
  Hand amended the values to make it compute.
- **I.4.7 — the six malefics**: Saturn, Mars; Mercury with them or in their nature; the Sun with
  Mars (conjunction/square/opposition, "or in the sextile or trine aspect of an infortune") —
  "two naturally bad, two accidentally so, two by place (the Head and Tail)". Fn 113: Bonatti reads
  the Head as malefic with malefics, the Tail with benefics.
- **I.4.8 — the ten killers**: Saturn, Mars, the Sun by opposition/square/conjunction; the Moon
  from the Sun's square or opposition, or the Ascendant degree to her body; "the cloudy and dark
  places"; **Antares and Aldebaran**; Mercury with the malefics; the 7th cusp; and **[10] "if the
  direction were completed at the end of a sign with half of the lesser or middle or greater years
  of the *kadukhudhāh*, it kills" (OCR, unverified)** — fn 123: source unknown, a minority Persian
  doctrine. A bound-to-bound transfer between two malefics' bounds is added.

## 3. TBN Book II (PDF pp. 49–71; printed 23–45) — annual methods

Dykes' summary of the apparatus (Intro §4, PDF p. 21) is better than the text: three named
**conditions** — the *general* (natal primary directions, a year per degree of ascension), the
*greater* (**30° profection increments treated as a compacted year, 12⅙ days per degree**), the
*lesser* (a point directed round the whole circle of the solar revolution at **59′ 08″ a day**) —
and for the parents, different points for each.

- **II.1.1** — projecting rays: Dorotheus' ascensional method (the ray's zodiacal degree converted
  to the ascensions of its sign, fn 129: less precise than semi-arcs); Figure 2 (lost). Ptolemy's
  method in a garbled paragraph (fn 132 explains what 'Umar is confusing); **the *hīlāj* between the
  MC and the setting degree "will be directed backwards … toward the seventh … a year for every
  degree, and it kills"**, referred to al-Qabīsī and Abū Ma'shar (fn 136: "an obvious clue that
  there are some later interpolations").
- **II.1.2** — the arithmetic: OA(*hīlāj*) and OA(malefic or its ray) from 0 Aries "by the
  ascensions of that city", subtract, a year per degree.
- **II.2 — the *jārbakhtār* and the three (four) managers.** "The distributor which is called the
  *jārbakhtār* (which is the Lord of the bound of the Ascendant, **whether it aspected the Ascendant
  or not**)" **(OCR, unverified)** — fn 145: in BA the *jārbakhtār* is the bound lord of the
  *hīlāj*; TBN, Abū Bakr I.17 and Abū Ma'shar direct the **Ascendant**. Second, "the lord of the
  rays" (fn 146: Dykes re-reads *diversionis* as *directionis* — simply the planet casting rays into
  the bound, "a standard part of the Persian annual procedure"). Third, the receiver, the next bound
  lord. All four bad → death; all four good → "the enlargement of life"; 3:1, 2:2 graded. Then a
  second list of four "in which there is trust": the distributor, **the Lord of the Year**, the lord
  of the rays, the receiver, with the profected sign commingled. Then the two directions of the
  revolution: the lesser condition daily (SR Ascendant, 59′ 08″ a day by equal degrees, round to its
  return; the planet whose ray is on the SR Ascendant degree disposes first, else the Ascendant
  itself until it applies) and the greater from the profected sign.
- **II.3** — mundane directions (kings from the MC by right ascensions at 59′ 08″ a day; the vulgar
  from the Ascendant); fn 158: repeated verbatim in al-Qabīsī IV.13. The 'Uthmān bin 'Affān
  anecdote. Skip.
- **II.4 — 'Umar's profection.** From the natal Ascendant a year per 30°; within the year's 30°,
  the distance to a fortune or infortune "multiplied by 12 and 1/6 of a full degree" gives days
  (fn 165: 365/30 = 12.166; the later refinement is × 12 ÷ 59′ 08″). Fn 164: the text reads
  "one-seventh", corrected to the sixth used below.
- **II.5 — the three conditions, native and parents**, disentangled by Dykes (fn 168). Native:
  general = natal Ascendant by ascensions "whether it were the *hīlāj* or not"; greater = profected
  30° at 12⅙ days/°; lesser = SR Ascendant at 59′ 08″/day. Father: general = the Lot of the Father
  by ascensions (fn 174: "or until one of the four actors aspected them"); greater = the Lot's
  profection; **lesser = "the degree of the 4th house [in the revolution]" at 59′ 08″/day**.
  Mother: general = the Lot of the Mother; greater = the same, a year per 30°; **lesser = the
  Moon's degree [in the revolution] at 59′ 08″/day**. The Māshā'allāh dissent (direct from the ray of
  the exaltation/triplicity/bound lord nearest the Ascendant) is rejected: "it can come about that
  some one of these four does not aspect the Ascendant" — hence II.8.
- **II.6 — three worked examples**, Figures 3–5 (lost; Dykes dates them c. 614, 642 and 622 AD,
  fns 188, 197, 204 — the Sassanian-original hypothesis of Intro §4). The lesser condition steps
  round the SR chart ray by ray, a day per degree (fn 191: 'Umar simplifies 59′ 08″ to 1°); the
  greater multiplies by 12 flat (fn 198) and runs past the year's end (fn 200); the general gives
  a year per ecliptic degree, ascensions ignored (fn 204). **Do not build a fixture on any of them.**
  Then the bound distribution restated: "look regarding the Lord of the bound of the Ascendant,
  because it is the *jārbakhtār*"; a planet that "signifies the detriment of a limb … will come with
  its own signification when it distributes".
- **II.7** — Ptolemy's annual directions: the Moon for body and mother, the Lot of Fortune for
  riches, the Sun for father and kingdom, the MC by right ascensions; the Ascendant "for knowing
  his condition, by means of the distributions of the bounds". Distributor/rays matrix: bad/bad,
  bad/good ("dual … infirmity with health, and poverty with abundance"), good/good relative to
  station of birth.
- **II.8** — rejection of Māshā'allāh's direction from the lord of the Ascendant's ray; "it is
  altogether necessary to direct from the Ascendant". Fn 211: Dykes has not seen this in BA.

## 4. TBN Book III (PDF pp. 72–104; printed 46–78) — topics

The template, once: places `[1]…[n]`, the *mubtazz* over them, its concord with the *mubtazz* of
the Ascendant "from the fourteen ways which signify effecting and destruction" (fn 242–243: Sahl's
*Introduct.* §5 configurations), the triplicity lords of the natural significator for the three ages.

- **III.1.1 — prosperity, four methods.** [1] *dusturiyyah* ("security", "rightness"): diurnal
  planets oriental from the Sun, nocturnal occidental from the Moon; superiors in domicile/exaltation
  with the Sun in his, angular, mutually aspecting → king (fn 219: garbled, from *Tet.* IV.3 and
  Rhetorius 23, "mixes elements from all three types of *dusturiyyah*"). [2] **Dorotheus' 15° bands**
  from the triplicity lord of the sect light: "the degrees which are between the degree of the
  planet and the degree of the angle … first 15° → prince or like a king; next 15° → noble; third
  15° → middling; beyond → hard at labor" **(OCR, unverified)** — *Carmen* I.26, 2–4; **degrees
  unspecified as zodiacal or ascensional.** [3] the Book of Alleypms's four types (kings who last;
  labourers who last; paupers made rich; rich made poor) with the lord of the Ascendant, the Moon
  and the Sun angular/cadent and their connections. [4] Dorotheus and Hermes: the first planet to
  reach the Moon's or Ascendant's degree after birth (fn 232: by transit, direction or profection
  unclear). [5] Hermes' lord of the year = first planet to change figure. [6–7] the Moon's speed
  changing; a void Moon = "a wild man, of the sects of the desert".
- **III.1.2 — three methods of prediction.** The triplicity lords of the sect light for the three
  ages; **Ptolemy's twelve-place life map** (12th/1st/2nd = beginning/middle/end of the first age;
  9th/10th/11th the middle age; 6th/7th/8th the end; 3rd/4th/5th death); the Moon's applications by
  number.
- **III.2 wealth** — 2nd, planets in it, lord, Lot of Substance and lord, Jupiter, Fortune and
  lord, *mubtazz*; "the *mubtazz* [is the one] which was more worthy and authoritative than the rest
  by a multitude of dignities" **(OCR, unverified)** — a count, no weights; oriental → youth,
  occidental → age; the *mubtazz*'s three triplicity lords for the three ages.
- **III.3 siblings** — 3rd, lord, planets, Lot of Brothers, lord, **Mars and his triplicity lords**
  (fn 250: Abū 'Alī uses the 3rd's); planets between MC and Ascendant = elder siblings, between
  Ascendant and IC = younger, doubled in common signs; the bracketed planet-to-sibling key (fn 253,
  from BA/Carmen); the Ascendant's strongest triplicity lord in the ASC/MC/7th/4th → first, fourth,
  seventh child.
- **III.4.1 the father** — 4th, lord, [Lot of Fathers, lord, Saturn and the Sun] (fn 257: **added
  from Abū Bakr II.5.10 and 5.14**), *mubtazz*, planets in the 4th; the *mubtazz* treated as a
  *kadukhudhāh* for the father's years; direct the Lot of the Father, the Sun or the 4th-house degree
  ("whichever was more worthy to be the *hīlāj*"); *dusturiyyah* of the Sun by day / of Saturn from the
  Sun by night; triplicity lords of the Sun (day) / Saturn (night); severity of death from Mars (day)
  / Saturn (night) impeding the *mubtazz*; illegitimacy if the Sun applies to Mars by day (Saturn by
  night), the mother suspect if the Moon does; "if the Lord of the house of fathers did not aspect
  the house of children [fn 269: Abū Bakr reads 'its own domicile'], nor the Sun the Lord of his own
  domicile, nor the Lord of the Lot of the Father the Lot, the native will not be the son of his
  father".
- **III.4.2 the mother** — **the tenth sign**, its lord, the Lot of the Mother and lord, Venus [and
  the Moon]. **Fn 270: "The attribution of the tenth sign to the mother is not standard and seems to
  come from a misreading of *Tet.* III.6 … a main source for this attribution in later Western
  astrology" (OCR, unverified).** *Dusturiyyah* of the nocturnal planets from the Moon by night, of
  Venus from the Moon by day; direct the Moon or the Lot, whichever is *hīlāj*, else the Moon
  (fn 279: JN reads "if you did *not* find a *hīlāj*"); Dorotheus' rule for which parent dies first
  (the significator that sets under the earth sooner).
- **III.5 marriage** — 7th, lord, planets, Moon, Venus, Lot of Marriage-Union, lord, *mubtazz*;
  Ptolemy's Moon in the eastern quarters (redefined, fn 287) → early marriage; Dorotheus' triplicity
  lords of Venus; Saturn overcoming Venus from the tenth → "numbed by women"; the sexual-conduct
  rules (Venus with Mars in his dignity; Mercury *mubtazz* over Venus, Moon, 7th and the Lot → boys;
  planets' genders over the Ascendant, luminaries, Fortune and syzygy → effeminacy/manliness).
- **III.6 children** — 5th, lord, Venus and Jupiter, Lot of Children (fn 304: Jupiter–Saturn), lord,
  *mubtazz*; concord with the *mubtazz* of the Ascendant *or of the Moon*; **sterile signs "Leo,
  Taurus, Capricorn, Libra, and Aquarius"** (fn 308 lists the four rival lists); oriental → youth;
  Jupiter/Venus many, Saturn/Mars few, luminaries middling, Mercury with whichever; Ptolemy's 11th.
- **III.7 infirmities** — 6th, lord, planets, Mercury, Lot of Infirmity (Saturn→Mars by day, fn 324)
  and lord, *mubtazz*; Saturn cold-dry (epilepsy, dropsy, gout), Mars choler and blood; the
  luminaries' eyes rules (right eye from the Sun, left from the Moon); the Moon in Cancer, Leo,
  Scorpio, Sagittarius, Capricorn, Aquarius → eyes (fn 333: Rhetorius 61); waning in Sagittarius →
  blindness; the eunuch rule; a garbled dwarf sentence (fn 340).
- **III.8 travel** — 9th, lord, planets, Mars, Lot of Foreign Travel, lord, *mubtazz*; Mars angular;
  **the Moon not aspecting her domicile lord → livelihood abroad**; fn 342: JN Ch. 27 reproduces
  four paragraphs verbatim.
- **III.9 work and mastery** — MC, lord, Sun, Lot of Work, lord, *mubtazz*; the Sun in eastern
  quarters → honour in youth; the Sun's triplicity lords; **Saturn angular by night / Mars by day
  in ASC or MC → no work or kingdom until the planet's lesser years pass** (fn 354); the substance of
  the *mubtazz* (Mercury → scribe/astrologer, Sun → prince); Ptolemy's morning-rising planet or
  MC lord; **Mars, Venus, Mercury for the substance of the mastery**; the MC falling in the 11th
  raises, in the 9th lowers, "the distributor's dignity".
- **III.10 friends** — 11th, lord, Venus, Lot of Friends, planets; movable signs → friends do not
  last; the three-way synastry from *Tet.* IV.7 (luminaries in the same or exchanged signs;
  Ascendants configured; Lots of Fortune in one sign or triplicity); Saturn's aspects between two
  charts.
- **III.11 enemies** — 12th, lord, Lot of Enemies (fn 394: Gr. Intr. VIII.4 gives two), lord,
  Saturn, planets; "the native will hate that sign and that image of the circle".
- **III.12 faith** — 9th, 3rd, their lords and planets, Lot of Faith, lord, *mubtazz*; a safe
  Saturn (or any superior) → "worshippers of one God"; Mercury lord of the 9th agreeing with the
  lord of the Ascendant → a disputer; the triplicity lords of the Lot of Faith for the age of best
  faith.
- **III.13 death** — Ptolemy's *Tet.* IV.9 by constellation (Ophiuchus, Hydra, the birds, the
  quadrupeds, Perseus/Algol), by hemisphere (above → crucifixion; below → falls, shipwreck, bodies
  not found) and by element of the *mubtazz* over death.

## 5. Abū Bakr (PDF pp. 105–359; printed 79–333)

### 5a. Book I (PDF pp. 105–164) — overview, pregnancy, longevity

- **I.1.0–1.2** — the syllabus: what to verify about the planets (fn 431: "a straightforward appeal
  to quadrant houses"; fn 433–34: "binding" = application within orbs, *iqbāl*/*idbār* = angular/
  cadent, both glossed to Sahl's *Introduct.* §5); the signs' properties, including "the *zamīn*
  [chronic-illness] degrees and the welled ones", the "*biyābānīya* degrees (that is, those of the
  fixed stars of the first or second magnitude)"; the Moon's conditions including her position
  "through the 40 days before or after the nativity". Then the questionnaire of every house, the
  fullest such list in the corpus.
- **I.1.3** — prediction: the *hīlāj* and *kadukhudhāh* for the lifespan; **the triplicity lords for
  the three parts of life; the bound lord ("which is called the 'distributor'") for fortune and
  labor**; and *tasyīr* (direction) of the *hīlāj*, Sun (loftiness, father, elder brothers,
  kingdom), Moon (illness, marriage), Fortune, Ascendant (life, travel), 10th degree (works,
  mastery), Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Venus, each with a topic.
- **I.2, I.5** — the seed and the seven months, greatly expanded from TBN I.1 with the dispositor's
  condition in each month (in whose bound; ascending/descending in the epicycle, at the apogee
  (*awj*) or its opposite; with the Tail) read as the native's future character and body. I.5's
  Venus month is by planetary hour of the projection (fn — the day's twelve hours divided among the
  faces). Fn 490: "the astrological equivalent of genetics".
- **I.3** — the stay in the womb: the Sun a sign per month; the 5th sign explains the 5th house of
  children (same element as the Ascendant); fetal orientation by sex (fn 478 doubts it).
- **I.4 — the *namūdār***: **I.4.1 the Trutine of Hermes** (Moon under the earth: ASC→Moon
  counter-clockwise, doubled, ÷ 24 → days and hours added to 273; above the earth: DSC→Moon, added
  to 258; the greater stay 288); I.4.2 a second method Dykes "confesses" not to understand (fn 485);
  I.4.3 the Moon directed a year before and after birth, the three places' aspects → nine, eight or
  seven months (fn 489: "could Abū Bakr simply mean the transiting Moon?").
- **I.6** — legitimacy: the Lot of the Father aspecting the Lot of Fortune with reception; the Moon
  with the Sun or under his rays → "furtive, and from adultery". Sahl fn 8 (4.1) cites this.
- **I.7** — quick or slow birth (the Moon's condition; Saturn stationary in an angle "perhaps the
  woman will not give birth until [the native] touches death").
- **I.8** — monstrous births: the twelfth-parts of the seven planets in bestial signs; Hermes'
  compound rule; **Figures 6–7 (Dykes' own diagrams, fn 516; fn 517: chart #1 is impossible and
  undatable; fn 522: #2 is Alexandria, 19 June 719 AD)** — a dog and a donkey.
- **I.9–I.11** — colour and face: the ancients' dispute; al-Andarzaghar (fn 531, transliterated
  *Andoroar/Amdasoar* — Sahl fn 406 cites this); the 24,000-moments doctrine (fn 532: from BA I.1);
  **the face lord of the Ascendant as the colour significator, "provided however that it aspects the
  Ascendant"**; planets' and signs' colours; likeness to father (Sun the face lord) or mother (Moon).
- **I.12 — nourishing.** The four types restated with **eight significators** (Ascendant, lord,
  Fortune, lord, Sun, Moon, conjunction, prevention) and the triplicity lords "in their strength and
  weakness, and also the *iqbāl* and *idbār*". Type 1 (born dead), 2 (not nourished), 3 (not to old
  age), 4 (old age), each a page of configurations, fn 573 explaining a sect rule: **"Mars destroys
  nourishing, especially in diurnal nativities, and especially if he were above the earth" (OCR,
  unverified)** — the malefic contrary to sect on the side of the horizon he disprefers. **I.12.5
  the Moon's days** (3rd, 7th, 40th, fn 579 the whole-sign reversal on the 40th) and the ages
  3/7/40 years. **I.12.6 the faces at birth and the timing of infant illness** — a table of the 36
  faces with months and years, half of them missing in both witnesses (fn 585: "mismatched"; Dykes
  prints 1540's).
- **I.13–I.14** — abandonment; beauty of nourishment; **the Lot of Absence (= Spirit): "taken in
  the day from the Moon to the Sun, and in the night the reverse, and it is projected from the
  Ascendant" (OCR, unverified)**.
- **I.15 — the *hīlāj* and *kadukhudhāh*.** Five places; **the Sun by day in the Ascendant, 10th or
  11th in any sign, in the 7th, 8th or 9th only masculine; the Moon by night in the same places
  feminine**; Ptolemy's 4th allowed (fn 603: copying 'Umar's error); then "the degree of the
  Ascendant … free from the infortunes, and its Lord strong"; then the Lot of Fortune; then the
  syzygy. "Certain sages": sect-swap — by day the Moon in the Ascendant/angles/dignified/feminine; by
  night the Sun in the 4th, 8th or 5th. Dorotheus: the Lot in the Ascendant past 25° from the
  angle; luminaries in the 9th if in their own places. **For the Ascendant, Lot and syzygy "we
  should not look to see whether it is a masculine or feminine sign, or whether the nativity is
  diurnal or nocturnal, but whether their Lords aspect the *hīlāj* itself."** No aspecting lord → no
  *kadukhudhāh* → direct the Ascendant from the infortunes. **Preference: "the Lord of the domicile of
  the *hīlāj* … over the Lord of the exaltation; and the Lord of the triplicity to the Lord of the
  bound, and the Lord of the bound to the Lord of the face. Therefore the planet which had more
  dignities … is more fit" (OCR, unverified)** — domicile-first, the reverse of TBN I.4.2 and Sahl
  1.20, 2. **The years grid** (PDF pp. 154–155): 10th or 1st strong and dignified → greater;
  peregrine there → middle; retrograde/burned/unfortunate → lesser; **4th, 5th or 11th dignified →
  lesser**; there retrograde/burned/fall → months; 6th, 12th, 9th strong and received → months; there
  impeded → days or hours. Aspecting planets: in ASC/10th/7th → their lesser years; in 5th/10th/4th
  dignified → lesser; retrograde/burned → months; and so on. A second, later grid (PDF pp. 155–156,
  "certain sages") is inconsistent with the first (angular, occidental, dignified → middle; in the
  9th oriental dignified → middle; 3rd "less strong than the 9th"; 6th/12th → lesser, weakened →
  months, retrograde/peregrine → days, burned → hours). **Al-Ḥasan's own rule**: a *kadukhudhāh*
  pushing to a strong planet that receives it and does not return the light — take that planet
  instead (fn 613 gives Jag.'s different sentence). **The nodes, twice and contradictorily**: "If the
  Head of the Dragon were with the *kadukhudhāh*, it will subtract one-fourth of the years" (PDF
  p. 156), then "'Thābit' [fn 620: Jag. reads *Dalbarih*, i.e. al-Ṭabarī] said: … with the Head …
  it will add one-fourth … with the Tail, it subtracts" (PDF p. 157) **(both OCR, unverified)**.
  "Retrograde planets, because of the weakness of their significations, do not make good nor evil."
  Infortunes reaching the *kadukhudhāh*'s dominion from the angles by more than a body's width add,
  not subtract, but "unite the native to innumerable dangers". A Lot-of-Fortune method for those
  without a *kadukhudhāh* (fn 622: *Anth.* III.11, BA III.1.7). **"If the ascending sign were 29°
  … the Lord of the second will be a partner with the Lord of the Ascendant in that year, and in
  the following year he alone" (OCR, unverified)**, fn 623: reflected in Morin.
- **I.16 — the planetary years** (PDF pp. 159–160). The greater years of the five "from the bounds
  of the planets" (sum of Egyptian bound-degrees); **the Sun's 120 = 10 (his *fardār*) × 12, the
  Moon's 108 = 9 × 12; the luminaries' middle years "by the halving of their greater years and the
  addition of their lesser years, and the halving of all of them assembled together" — 120/2 = 60,
  + 19 = 79, /2 = 39½** (fn 625: Dykes does not know the source and calls 69½ "traditional"). Lesser
  years rationalised: Sun 19 = degrees from 0 Aries to his exaltation; Moon 25 = days visible;
  Mercury 20 = days hidden; Saturn 30 and Jupiter 12 = orbital periods; Mars 15 = months Aries to
  Scorpio (fn 629: "only occasionally true"); Venus 8 = "her own circle in eight months" (fn 630:
  "not true"). The five planets' middle years = (lesser + greater)/2.
- **I.17 — the distributor.** The bound lord of the Ascendant's degree, a year per degree "in
  oblique ascensions" (fn 632), bound to bound to the end of life; **mix the aspecting planets'
  significations with the bound lord's**; the worked Mercury-bound example (Jupiter square, Venus
  trine, Saturn and Mars opposed) and the caution that a bound disposing an infant cannot give
  "commerce" or "wives" but only precocity and beauty — **"we must consider the type of the native
  and his age"**; the rays into the bound timed by degree ("if Mars projected his own rays upon the
  second degree of the bound, say … hot illnesses in the beginning of the second year"); the
  1 Gemini example with 5° of Mercury's bound = 4° 40′ of ascension = four years eight months
  (fn 639: 45° N, the middle of his table). Fn 631 cross-references *Carmen* III.1, BA IV, TBN
  II.2/II.6 and PN IV Chs. 2.4ff.

### 5b. Book II (PDF pp. 165–359) — topics

Fourteen topics, each opening with a places list and "the planet which conjoins the testimonies of
these places" (his phrase for the *mubtazz*; he never gives weights), then chapters of
configurations. The apparatus, chapter by chapter where it matters:

- **II.1.0 morals** — the seven planets' natural moral significations (Saturn "laziness, stupidity,
  foulness, fear, servitude"; Mars "wrath, disgust, violence"; Venus "humbleness, piety … except
  that the native will be reputed to be impertinent and effeminate"; the Moon "a heavy tread,
  weakness, misery, travels"); strength by place; the aspecting planet alters; then Mars in the
  Ascendant or a strong place with each aspecting planet in turn (fn 646: joined to the Moon or lord
  of the Ascendant, "an important point"); the *biyābānīya* star at 2 Taurus (fn 649: Pollux);
  **"Mars in a nocturnal nativity, appearing in his own domicile, signifies that the native will be
  one master … a good soldier … And if he were in his own domicile in a diurnal nativity, the
  native will be lazy in those things in which he ought to make money; infirm, greedy, an evil
  plunderer of strangers, a fornicator, esteeming murders and evils, violent and unsound" (PDF
  p. 168, OCR, unverified)** — the course's "Abū Bakr suggests that Mars can be lazy". Mars in the
  MC "would rejoice in his own place" → fortunate in war. The Lot of Boldness (fn 653: Hermetic Lot
  of Mars).
- **II.1.1–1.33** — thirty-three character chapters (anxiety; rage; humbleness; shamelessness;
  shame; beauty of morals; lies — with the *biyābānīya* stars of Mars-Mercury nature, fn 664;

- **II.2.0 prosperity** — seven categories (complete; middling; miserable; changing; raised;

  its Lord the end" (OCR, unverified)**; the triplicity lords "direct, in each other's domicile, and

- **II.3 wealth** — great wealth; administrators; the wise; commerce; slaves; beasts; masters of

- **II.4 siblings** — 3rd and lord *and its triplicity lords*, Lot of Brothers, Fortune, all seven

2. **The sect delineations of Lesson 10 — Mars "lazy". Class 1.** Abū Bakr II.1.0 (PDF p. 168): Mars
   in his own domicile by night → "one master … a good soldier … always conquering"; by day → "lazy

   unsound". The course's line is a fair gloss, but the source's condition is **Mars in his domicile,

## 8. What this volume adds to the corpus that nothing else had

1. **The origin of the weighted victor**, with its numbers, its inventor (al-'Anbas, or Jag.'s
   "Aseme-zael") and its date window (815–844), and Dykes' verdict that TBN itself does not weight.
   The corpus had the tables (*Handy Tables* p. 33) and no provenance.
2. **The 'Umar annual apparatus in its own words** — three conditions, the 30° increment, the SR
   Ascendant at 59′ 08″ a day (an earlier witness than PN IV IX.7), the four managers of a bound,
   the parents' points in the revolution.
3. **The fullest *hīlāj*/*kadukhudhāh* parallel to Sahl 1.15–1.21** — TBN I.4.1–4 and Abū Bakr
   I.15 — including the sentences that resolve or condition Sahl's unclear ones (§7.3, §7.4).
4. **Abū Bakr's planetary-years chapter** (I.16): the luminaries' middle years constructed in prose.
5. **A second stars-harming-eyes list** (Māshā'allāh's, through Abū Bakr II.7.3).
6. **The sect line behind Lesson 10's Mars** (II.1.0), and the sect-of-malefic rule of I.12.3.
7. **The mastery apparatus** (II.12.2–12.8, 12.46) and the Moon-application override.
8. **The character and illness catalogues** — II.1 and II.7 — which no other text in hand has at
   this grain; Dykes: "the most complete astrological account of illness I have seen".
9. **Vocabulary**: *namūdār*, *biyābānīya*, *zamīn* degrees, *dusturiyyah* defined as "security",
   the Lot of Absence, "binding", *iqbāl/idbār*, the "fourteen ways".

## 9. What it does not add

- **No weighted victor in TBN.** Anyone hoping to cite 'Umar's own text for 5-4-3-2-1 will not find
  it; the count is Ptolemaic and the weights are reported second-hand in another author.
- **No revolution chart construction, no *fardārs* (TBN omits them, Intro PDF p. 20), no
  monthly revolutions, no cutters list.** PN IV owns all of that.
- **No sentence numbers, no figures**, and three worked charts Dykes could not make compute.
- **Nothing on the Moon's twelve phases, the seven planetary Lots, Ptolemy's elemental powers,
  bonification/maltreatment, Morin, or the eastern/western quadrants by age** — `COURSE_COVERAGE`
  gaps 2–8 and 12 stand untouched.
- **No Arabic.** Both texts are Latin-derived; every technical term is a Latin transliteration and
  Dykes' footnotes reconstruct the Arabic behind it. Where Sahl's Arabic and this Latin differ
  (fn 156), the Latin does not settle it.

## 10. Reading notes

- **Dykes rearranged TBN and says exactly where** (fns 24, 30, 36, 74, 79, 84, 103, 110, 115, 138,
  139, 160, 167, 175, 283, 405, 409). A citation to TBN by Hand/Schmidt's 1997 numbering will not
  match this edition; cite Dykes only.
- **Jag. vs 1540 is the volume's running argument.** 1540 intensifies conditions ("peregrine and
  made unfortunate in the sixth" for Jag.'s "in the sixth", Intro PDF p. 25), says "houses" where
  Jag. says "signs", and has whole chapters Jag. lacks (II.1.9–1.15, II.9.2, II.9.18) and vice versa
  (II.7.0, II.7.38, most of II.5.7, II.12.3). Where a delineation matters, check which witness
  carries it before quoting.
- **The Introduction's editorial verdicts** — the weighted victor is "misguided and artificial";
  the Moon-application secondary aspect is to the primary planet, not the Moon; the ninth-parts
  and the like — are Dykes', signposted, and not doctrine. The same rule as PN IV.
- **The OCR's worst pages are the figure pages and the footnote-dense ones** (PDF pp. 45–46, 50,
  62–68, 131–133, 365–367). Read the body first; the footnote text beneath a figure is often
  unrecoverable.
- **The victor question has a documentary trail across three files**: Intro PDF pp. 15–17, fn 10,
  fn 48, Abū Bakr II.5.14 with fns 955–958, and `course/README.md`'s 2026-09-13 confirmation of the
  Handy Tables. Anyone ruling on §7.1 should read all five before deciding what "Older method"
  should be called.
