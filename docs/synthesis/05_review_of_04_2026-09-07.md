# Tier 1

## R1. "Three definitions of spear-bearing, two of them incompatible" — **REFUTED**

### R1.1 What Ch. 2.5 says — CONFIRMED as quoted, with two things the report drops

The ¶2–3 quotation is verbatim (`on_nativities.md:3614`). Two features the report does not mention:

- **"or one of its shares"** (`:3614`) widens the dignity condition beyond house/exaltation to any
  share — and fn. 122 on ¶3 reads: *"حظ, which normally means 'share' or 'dignity.'"* (`:3624`).
  That footnote is attached to the word Dykes renders **"sect"** in ¶3. The editor's introduction
  says the same thing at length: *"Sahl's Arabic frequently refers to dignity as a 'share' (حظ) …
  But occasionally Sahl uses this word when he really means sect"* (`sahl_editors_introduction.md:756`).
  So "lesser grade = same sect" is a translator's choice the apparatus itself hedges; the lesser grade
  may equally be "both having a share [dignity] by day / by night" (i.e. triplicity by sect). The
  report presents it unhedged. **UNVERIFIABLE which reading is right; the report should carry both.**

### R1.2 "Definition III explicitly excludes an aspect" — REFUTED

The report rests this on Ch. 10.2.7, 3: *"without a connection from him to them"* (`:12352`).

- Sahl's own glossary: **"Connection** … When a planet **applies** to another planet (by body in the
  same sign, or by ray in configured signs), **within a particular number of degrees** up to
  exactness" (`sahl_glossary.md:93`); **"Configured.** To be in an aspect by whole-sign (**though not
  necessarily connecting by degree**)" (`:90`). "Without a connection" means *no application by
  degree*; it says nothing about whole-sign configuration. `app.py` keeps the same two concepts apart
  (`_is_connected_sahl`/`_is_connected_abu_mashar` at 1274/1367 vs `_pairwise_configurations` at 985,
  `configured_to` at 5116).
- The examples themselves include a connection: **"So his good fortune was the easternization of
  Venus alone, through a connection"** — Ch. 10.2.7, 20 (`:12442`). Example #1's "without a
  connection" is descriptive ("even without"), not a criterion. fn. 191 reads it the same way: *"they
  are close enough that they do not make a morning rising out of the rays."*

### R1.4 Locations the report missed — the corpus states the framing the report calls absent

The report says the doctrine "touches at least eight places" in `on_nativities.md`. Four other
files touch it, and two of them settle the framing question:

- **Sahl's glossary**: *"Spear-bearing … A special configuration in a chart showing eminence and
  prosperity, **of which there were several types and definitions**. Spear-bearing requires that
  there be a **royal planet** (usually, a luminary), which is accompanied by a spear-bearing planet."*
  (`sahl_glossary.md:437`). fn. 129 at `:12005` points to exactly this entry ("See the Glossary").
- **Editor's introduction**, a whole section *"Right-siding, paying honor, spear-bearing"*:
  *"**different astrologers listed the various criteria** which could show spear-bearing … Being 'on
  the right' refers to the fact that spear-bearing planets for the Sun are often preferred to be
  eastern, rising before him (on his right). See Nativities Chs. 2.5 and 10.2.7."*
  (`sahl_editors_introduction.md:754`).
- **Abū Ma'shar VII.2, 17–19** — a *degree-banded, computable* statement of the eastern type:
  *"until there are 60° between them and the Sun … it is the most powerful it could be in
  easternization, **spear-bearing, and being on the right side** of the Sun. 19 Now when they have
  gone past these degrees … their right-siding for the Sun is weak, as well as their spear-bearing.
  20 And they do not cease to be like that until there come to be 90° between them and the Sun"*
  (`abu_mashar_book_vii.md:223–227`); fn. 32: *"All three meanings are connected here: being on the
  right side, bringing luck, and spear-bearing for the Sun."* Also VII.2, 12: emerging from burning,
  *"they are suitable for granting their greater years as well as spear-bearing"* (`:201`).
- **Appendix A §49**: *"One whose lord of the Ascendant is right-siding the Sun and has spear-bearing,
  and is **superior to [the Sun]**, and its easternization is accomplished, is of those who associates
  with kings"* (`sahl_appendix_a_sixty_six_sections.md:216`) — adds *superiority* as a further criterion.

### R1 verdict

**One doctrine, several traditional types, which the corpus itself says is the normal state of
affairs** — not three distinct doctrines, and not two incompatible ones. Ch. 2.5 = Antiochus's first
type (dignity + configuration; fn. 121 says so). Ch. 10.2.1 = Ptolemy's stake-ladder over the
eastern/western type. Ch. 10.2.7 = Persian examples of the eastern/western type. Abū Ma'shar VII.2 =
the same eastern type with degree bands. The types are cumulative; the report's "no textual
tiebreak" premise dissolves because nothing needs to be tied.
