## 1. Sahl's own statement of what elections can and cannot do (Ch. 1)

The most interesting chapter in the work, and the least electional. Sahl argues that **elections are
subordinate to the nativity**, and argues it from observation.

> "They have agreed that **choices are weak** except for kings: for truly, even if their choice is
> weak, [kings] have a **root** strengthening their nativities" — Choices Ch. 1, 3

> "beware of choosing for one whose **root** of his nativity or his question indicates something
> detestable, for truly [even] if you put **all of the fortunes in the stakes** and made the
> infortunes … fall away from it, **it would not benefit that man anything**" — Choices Ch. 1, 8

> "one is already warned of that based on the group of people who **sail on the sea or head out on a
> journey at one [and the same] hour**: for of them, **one sinks and another is safe** … **10** And I
> have already **tested that many times** … in truth, that comes from **their nativities and their
> distribution from those years**." — Choices Ch. 1, 9–10

An appeal to repeated observation, unusual in this corpus. Dykes's fn. 1 extracts the implied
hierarchy: kings who know their nativities > lower-class people who know theirs > someone with only
a question chart > someone with no chart at all, for whom Sahl says do not take the election.

### ⚠ 1a. Benefic/malefic is chart-relative — and Sahl contradicts himself four sentences later

> "perhaps you have seen the indicator being connected to an infortune from a square, assembly, or
> opposition, and **someone had gains**: **that infortune was good for him**, because the infortunes
> are perhaps **more fitting for him**, since [one] may be **the lord of the original Ascendant**,
> and the lord of the distribution, or the lord of the Ascendant of the year." — Choices Ch. 1, 12

(fn. 8: "original" = natal.)

Against:

> "The fortunes are balanced and tempered in nature, while **the infortunes are unjust in nature**,
> and they want injustice: so beware of them. **17** For if they accepted [the management], **there
> is no escape from their injustice**" — Choices Ch. 1, 16–17

Recorded, not resolved. The likely reconciliation — ¶12 is about *whose* chart it is, ¶16–17 about
what the planets are *in themselves* — is not stated by Sahl and I am not supplying it.

## 2. Sign natures (Ch. 2) — transmitted from Theophilus, not composed by Sahl

⚠ **Provenance.** fns. 9, 12, 15: *"Most of the items in 3 derive from **Theophilus, OVI Ch. 1.1**,
which itself is based on **Orphic material**"*; likewise ¶7 (OVI Ch. 1.3) and ¶9–11 (OVI Ch. 1.2).

**Convertible** (¶2–5): "indicate the **quick transformation of matters**, and nothing is made fixed
by them." **Fixed** (¶6–8): "appropriate for every work whose **endurance, length, and fixity** its
owner wishes." **Double-bodied** (¶9–11): "what is done of something will **recur often** … For
everything of good and bad that befalls a man in them, will be **doubled** upon him."

### 2a. Intra-quadruplicity gradations — nothing else in the corpus has these

> "the **quickest of the convertible** [ones] are **Aries and Cancer** … and **Libra and Capricorn**
> are the more powerful and balanced of them." — Choices Ch. 2, 5

> "**Scorpio is the lightest of the fixed** ones, **Leo the most powerful in fixedness**, **Aquarius
> the most sluggish**, and **Taurus more even**." — Choices Ch. 2, 8

⚠ **¶5 conflicts with the corpus's own crooked/straight rule**, and Dykes says so at fn. 11:

> "In terms of crookedness and straightness (that is, ascensional times), **this is not true. Aries
> and Capricorn are crooked (and therefore faster), and Cancer and Libra are straight** … But Aries
> and Cancer could be viewed as faster because they are **ruled by Mars and the Moon** … See
> *Questions* Ch. 9, 78-79, where the Māshā'allāh version of that passage confirms that **the
> quickness of the lord contributes to it**."

Dykes's reconciliation (quickness of the *ruling planet*, not ascensional quickness) is his, not
Sahl's. Do not encode it.

### 2b. Sect applied

> "The **signs of the day** and a work of the day, are **stronger by day**; and [so] you should make
> the Ascendant be diurnal, and [make] the Moon be in the diurnal signs." — Choices Ch. 2, 13

## 3. General rules embedded in the topical chapters

Chs. 3–13 are per-house elections. Most of the content is topical and has no natal use. These are
the statements that are **general in form** — claims about aspect quality, place strength, solar
phase and planetary condition that hold regardless of the topic.

### 3a. Aspect quality — "friendship," and the square as middling

Sahl repeatedly names trine and sextile jointly as "**friendship**," and grades the square between
them and the opposition:

> "Let the Moon be received **from a trine or sextile** so that their **parting will be good**,
> because the aspect of the **square and opposition** [indicate that] there will be **words** between
> them at their parting, and **an aspect of friendship** indicates the goodness of their parting"
> — Choices Ch. 3, 10

> "let the time … be when the luminaries are **looking at each other from a trine or sextile**, so
> that there is **friendship and agreement** … **41** And as for the aspect of the **square, that is
> middling**; and the aspect of the **opposition** indicates that the slave will **argue with** his
> master." — Choices Ch. 6, 40–41

> "let the Moon be connecting with the lord of her house **from friendship (that is, from a trine or
> sextile)**" — Choices Ch. 4, 11

**Three-grade scale, stated repeatedly**: trine/sextile good · square middling · opposition hostile.
This matches Appendix B ¶17 and *Introduction* Ch. 2, 58 ("the square is the middle of the aspect
[of the opposition], not openly proclaiming hostility"). **Now attested in three separate works.**

⚠ **One counter-instance**, and it is explicit:

> "let the Moon be increasing in light, **connected to Jupiter from a square, for that is preferable
> to the opposition** and indicates the goodness of that building and its completion."
> — Choices Ch. 4, 6

Consistent with "middling" — better than opposition, worse than trine — but note it is a **square
used positively**, which a scoring engine that treats squares as simply harmful would misread.

### 3b. ★ A third good-places ranking, and it matches the glossary's hedge

> "let the Sun be in **an excellent place, in the Ascendant, eleventh, or tenth**. **13** And if he
> was **falling**, you will not get good from that; but if he was in the **ninth, third, or fifth**,
> it indicates **hardship and a scarcity of benefit**, and likewise the **stake of the west and the
> fourth** indicate a **scarcity of benefit, hardship, and prolonging**." — Choices Ch. 9, 12–13

So a three-grade scale:

| Grade | Places |
|---|---|
| **Excellent** | **1, 10, 11** |
| Hardship / scarcity | 9, 3, 5 — and also **7 and 4** |
| Falling | "you will not get good" |

**This corroborates the glossary's parenthetical hedge** at *Excellent place* (p. 780):

> "Includes several of the advantageous places, among which the **Ascendant, Midheaven, and eleventh
> are consistently mentioned**. **(These may be the only excellent places.)**"

The hedge now has a text behind it. Note that ¶13 puts the **7th and 4th** — both stakes — in the
*lower* grade, which no other scheme in the corpus does. That is a real datum against reading
"angular = strong" as uniform.

**This is now the fourth good-place scheme in the corpus**, alongside *Introduction* Ch. 2's
8-place and 7-place systems and Ch. 3, 78's six. See `01_introduction_ch1_ch2.md` §9 and
`02_reconciliation.md` §1.2.

### 3c. The Moon is more sensitive to affliction than the Ascendant

> "beware of putting the Moon with the infortunes or [making her] inspect them from a square or
> opposition, because **the infortunes' looking at the Ascendant is easier than their looking at the
> Moon**." — Choices Ch. 9, 8

A stated precedence between the two chief significators. Compare *Questions* Ch. 1, 22's tie-break
(prefer whichever of the lord of the Ascendant and the Moon is angular and aspects the Ascendant) —
different question, but the same pairing.

### 3d. Rising/declining × eastern/western, and direct/retrograde — two 2×2 matrices

Stated twice, for different topics, with the same structure:

> "let the lord of the Ascendant be **rising, eastern**. **23** For if it was **rising** and it was
> **not eastern**, it would be **quick in growth but there would be a delay in its bearing**. **24**
> And if it was **eastern [but] declining**, it would be **slow in growth [but] quick in bearing**.
> **25** And if it was **western [and] declining**, it would be **slow in growth and slow in
> bearing**." — Choices Ch. 4, 22–25

> "let the planet connecting with her be **direct, rising**, so that the animal will **increase in
> price and bodily [size]**: because if it was **rising [and] retrograde**, there will be a
> **decrease in the body** of the animal, **but with profit**. **5** And if it was **direct [but]
> declining**, it will **increase in body but the price will not be right**."
> — Choices Ch. 12, 4–5

These are the only places in the corpus I have found where **two condition axes are crossed
explicitly** and each of the four cells given a distinct outcome. Doctrinally that is a claim that
the axes are independent — which is what an engine that sums independent scores assumes, so this is
corroboration of the scoring *shape*, not just of individual rules.

### 3e. Solar-phase and "increasing/decreasing" used operationally

The chapters use, as live conditions, several quantities the engine computes:

- **increasing / decreasing in light** (Ch. 3, 2; Ch. 4, 6; Ch. 6, 19; Ch. 8, 3; Ch. 11, 6)
- **increasing / decreasing in calculation** (Ch. 4, 14, 28–29; Ch. 6, 19, 24–25; Ch. 8, 3)
- **increasing / decreasing in number** (Ch. 4, 28; Ch. 6, 14, 36; Ch. 11, 6)
- **under the rays / burning** (Ch. 3, 4–5; Ch. 4, 26; Ch. 9, 36; Ch. 12, 20)
- **eastern / western** (Ch. 4, 17, 22–25; Ch. 6, 37; Ch. 12, 2)
- **direct / retrograde** (Ch. 3, 15; Ch. 9, 18; Ch. 11, 6; Ch. 12, 4)
- **crooked / straight in rising** (Ch. 3, 18; Ch. 7, 22)
- **domain** (Ch. 7, 23)
- **empty in course** (Ch. 12, 9)
- **burned path** (Ch. 3, 6, 9)

Two are worth quoting because they are *uses* of doctrines the engine treats as data:

> "if the Moon was in the signs **straight in ascension**, increasing in light and number, connected
> to the fortunes, then whatever is bought at that time, its owner will lose it"
> — Choices Ch. 3, 18

> "do not ever travel to war unless Mars has governorship … and let him be in **signs straight in
> rising**." — Choices Ch. 7, 22

**Crooked/straight has a downstream consumer.** So does the burned path:

> "take care lest the Moon be in the belt of the signs or in the **burned path**, for indeed that is
> something detestable." — Choices Ch. 3, 6
> "the worst of them is **Libra (because the burned path is in it)**" — Choices Ch. 3, 9

⚠ Ch. 9, 41 gives a **degree** for it, obliquely: "in **Libra, if she went beyond 10°** you should
not set out by land nor by sea." And Ch. 9, 32: "beware travel … if the Moon is in the **last image
of Libra**." Neither is a definition of the burned path's span; 10° Libra matches **neither** of the
glossary's two candidate spans (15° Libra and 19° Libra). Recorded as a third, differently-framed
datum — **not** as a resolution.

### 3f. Venus's joy — presupposed, but never located

> "let Venus always be in her **exaltation, house, triplicity, or joy**, or the conjunction of
> Jupiter or Mercury" — Choices Ch. 7, 11

⚠ **This matters for the joys question.** `01_introduction_ch1_ch2.md` §9d found four joys located
in the corpus (9th/Sun, 3rd/Moon, 6th/Mars, 12th/Saturn), two of them weakly attested, and no joy
for Venus or Mercury. Ch. 7, 11 shows that **Sahl assumes Venus has a joy** and treats it as a
dignity-grade condition — but **does not say which place it is**.

So the correct statement is now: the corpus **locates** four joys and **presupposes** at least a
fifth without locating it. That is still not licence to supply the familiar 5th-house answer.

### 3g. The masculine/feminine quadrants used for sex selection

> "let the time of intercourse — the Ascendant and its lord, the Moon, and the lord of the house of
> children — be in **male signs or a masculine region of the circle** … **3** And if you wanted a
> feminine one, then let these indicators … be in the **female signs and a feminine region of the
> circle**." — Choices Ch. 5, 2–3

A use of the quadrant-gender doctrine of *Introduction* Ch. 3, 88 (testimony 88 in the code),
confirming the quadrants are a live condition and not merely a listed testimony.

### 3h. The Moon's solar-ray degrees, stated operationally

> "let the Moon at that time be with the Sun ⟨in⟩ what is between **12° up to 15°**; and that is
> better for the Moon, **until she goes out from under the rays up to 3°**" — Choices Ch. 9, 36

⚠ The passage is about concealment and is syntactically awkward, but it names **both 12° and 15°**
for the Moon in one sentence. The project already exposes a 12°/15° switch for `MOON_RAYS_ORB`
(`app.py:6433`) because Abū Ma'shar VII.2, 61 and Sahl *On Nativities* 1.19, 6 disagree. This is a
**third witness**, and it appears to use them as an inner and outer bound rather than as
alternatives. **I am not reading a rule out of it** — the sentence is too compressed and its subject
is elective concealment, not the definition of the rays. Recorded as relevant to that switch.

### 3i. Derived houses in a two-party election

> "the **Ascendant belongs to the one initiating** the partnership (or the younger of the two), and
> the **seventh belongs to the other partner**, and the **tenth** indicates what there will be
> between them, as well as the abundance of profit … and from the **fourth** you will understand the
> **outcome** of their matter." — Choices Ch. 3, 11

> "make the **second** and its lord suitable for **the assistants of the one beginning**, and the
> **eighth** and its lord for **the assistants of the enemy**" — Choices Ch. 7, 24

The 8th as "the 2nd from the 7th" — derived-house reasoning stated explicitly. Compare
*Introduction* Ch. 2, 21, which already gives the 8th "the allies of one who is sought."

## 4. What Chs. 3–13 do *not* contain

No figures. No dignity tables. No new aspect definitions. No timing procedure beyond what
*On Times* gives. The topical bulk — loans, partnerships, building, planting, medicine by body
region, marriage, war, travel by land and sea, slaves, animals, hunting, letters — is electional
judgment with no natal application, and is not synthesized here beyond the general rules above.
