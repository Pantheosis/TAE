## 0. The frame — sixteen approaches

> "**2** Know that the whole of what the stars indicate concerning what comes to be and is
> corrupted, is based on 16 approaches. **3** And they are: [1] advancement, [2] retreat, [3]
> connection, [4] separation, [5] transfer, [6] collection, [7] blocking, [8] reception, [9]
> <non-reception>, [10] emptiness of the course, [11] returning, [12] handing over power, [13]
> handing over management and nature, [14] strength, [15] weakness, and [16] the conditions of the
> Moon." — Intro Ch. 3, 2–3 (`:10`)

fn. 52: *"All manuscripts omit [non-reception], but it is clearly discussed later and is the only
way to reach 16."* Abū Ma'shar's twenty-one (VII.5) are a superset with a different cut: he splits
blocking into blocking/cutting/revoking/resistance/escape and adds wildness, reflection, two
natures, favor and recompense; he has no separate "advancement/retreat" or "conditions of the Moon"
in that list (they are in VII.3 and VII.6).

## 1. ★ Advancement and retreat (4–5, Fig. 9)

> "**4** As for [1] advancement, it is if a planet was in a stake or what follows a stake. **5** And
> [2] retreat is if a planet was falling from the stakes." — Intro Ch. 3, 4–5 (`:12–14`)

Figure 9's caption: *"Advancing (gray) and retreating (white), understood dynamically."* This is
the sense `app.py` calls "Sahl 83" (§7), distinct from Abū Ma'shar's quadrant sense (VII.3, 2 /
VI.26, 3 / Fig. 90) — see `project_advancing_two_senses`.

## 2. ★ Connection, separation, and the lights (6–23)

> "**6** And [3] connection is if a light, quick star is going straightaway to a heavy star, and the
> light star is fewer in degrees than the heavy one, as long as the [light] planet is going towards
> the [heavy] planet, until it touches it and comes to be with it by degree, to the minute: and then
> it is said to be connected. **7** And as for when it comes to be with it to the minute, its
> connection has come to an end, and then it is in the position of two men under a single blanket.
> … **9** And the planet does not cease to be counted as being connected until it separates from the
> planet by a full degree. **10** Now as for when the two planets are united in one sign, a planet
> is not considered to be separated from a planet until the light one departs from the heavy one by
> one-half of its body—and that is its light." — Intro Ch. 3, 6–10 (`:20–32`)

⚠ **Two separation rules in adjacent sentences**: ¶9 a full degree (any connection); ¶10 half the
body (a union in one sign). Abū Ma'shar has 1′ (VII.5, 16, 34) and the half-body window for
*"in the nature of"* (VII.5, 18) — the same two-tier shape.

> "**13** Know that the body of the Sun is 30°, so one-half of them are in front of him and
> one-half of them behind him … **14** And the light of the Moon is 12°, in front of her and behind
> her. **15** And the light of Saturn and Jupiter (each one) is 9° in front of him and likewise
> behind him. **16** And Mars is 8° in front of him and likewise behind him. **17** And Venus and
> Mercury (each one of them) is 7° in front of it and likewise behind it. **18** So by the extent of
> these lights, they are connected one to the other." — Intro Ch. 3, 13–18 (`:34`)

**15 / 12 / 9 / 8 / 7 — identical to Abū Ma'shar's Figure 105.** fn. 57: manuscript B orders them
by planet, the others by size. ¶19: an aspecting planet is connected once its light strikes the
other's degree — **the applying planet's own light is the orb**, which is what makes Sahl's test
asymmetric (`app.py:1324`).

> "**20** And if a planet was at the end of a sign, not connecting with anything, and it has already
> struck into the next sign with its own light, then whatever planet was the first in that light, it
> is connected with it. **21** And if it was not in the sign, then it will not see it." — Intro
> Ch. 3, 20–21 (`:38–54`)

fn. 58: *"the sentence is emphasizing the existence of out-of-sign conjunctions by body. But it does
not support out-of-sign aspects."* ⚠ This is the one place Sahl and Abū Ma'shar flatly differ on
mechanics: VII.5, 14 calls the same case *"not counted as a 'connection by assembly'"*.

> "**22** … separating, it is if the degree of the light planet separates from the degree of the
> heavy one, and the light planet is in more degrees than the heavy one. **23** So, looking is from
> sign to sign, and connecting is from degree to degree: and this is the statement of
> Māshā'allāh." — Intro Ch. 3, 22–23 (`:56`)

## 3. Transfer and collection (24–30, Figs. 10–11)

> "**24** … transfer of light, it is if the light planet separates from the heavy planet, and is
> connecting with another: it forms an equivalence between them, and it transfers the nature of the
> first to the second one which it has already connected with." — Intro Ch. 3, 24 (`:58`)

Worked example ¶25–27: Virgo rising, marriage; Moon 10° Gemini, Mercury 8° Leo, Jupiter 13° Pisces;
Mercury and Jupiter in aversion (fn. 60: *"this could be considered a case of 'reflection'"*);
the Moon separates from Mercury and connects with Jupiter → *"victory in the matter based on the
assistance of messengers."* Only the direct hand-off; Abū Ma'shar's Type II (VII.5, 85) is absent.

> "**28** And [6] collection of light is if the lord of the Ascendant and the lord of the sought
> thing are connecting with a planet heavier than they, so it collects their power and takes their
> light and their natures." — Intro Ch. 3, 28 (`:76`)

Example ¶29–30: Libra rising, the Sultan; Venus 10° Aries, Moon 12° Taurus, Jupiter 15° Cancer in
the Midheaven collects both. fn. 62 → *Questions* Ch. 1, 27: the collector should look at the
topic's place.

## 4. ★ Blocking, three ways, and the precedence rule (31–48, Figs. 12–15)

fn. 64 names them: *"connections by aspect 'cut' each other, conjunctions 'intervene' (lit., 'come in
between'), and a conjunction 'nullifies' an aspect."*

> "**32** Of them, [the first] is 'cutting the light': and it is if there was a planet between the
> lord of the Ascendant and the lord of the sought thing, in fewer degrees than one of them, so the
> connection with it is before the connection [of the lord of the Ascendant] with the lord of the
> sought thing." — Intro Ch. 3, 32 (`:90`)

Example ¶33–34 (Fig. 12): Virgo rising; Mercury 10° Cancer, Jupiter 15° Pisces, **Mars 13° Aries**
cuts Mercury's light from Jupiter; Mars in the eighth → *"any corruption … would be in relation to
the dowry."* (The figures `app.py:2712` quotes are exactly these.)

> "**35** The second way is if there is a light planet and another, heavy one, and they are both in
> one sign, and the light one is connecting with the heavy one, and if another planet connected, in
> [their] union, with the heavy one, and it is less than the light one [by degree], then the former
> has already intervened between [the light planet] and the connection [with the heavy one]." —
> Intro Ch. 3, 35 (`:108`)

Example ¶36–37 (Fig. 13): Cancer rising; Moon 8°, Mars 10°, Saturn 12° Gemini. (Abū Ma'shar's
Fig. 130 uses this example because his own inverts the planets, VII.5 fn. 173.)

> "**38** And the third way is if a planet is united to a planet heavier than it, connecting to it,
> and another one connects to the heavy one by an aspect, and [by degree] it is less than the light
> one that is uniting. **39** So the uniting planet blocks the planet that is looking, from the
> connection. **40** And if it goes beyond that, its connection is valid." — Intro Ch. 3, 38–40
> (`:114`)

Example ¶42–43 (Fig. 14): Cancer rising; Moon 10° Scorpio, Mars 15° Taurus, Saturn 23° Taurus —
⚠ the Moon is **13°** from her opposition to Saturn, outside her own 12° light, and Sahl still
calls it *"cutting the aspect"*; `app.py:2158` records the inconsistency and honours it by one
degree.

> "**44** And a connection does not nullify a uniting, but a uniting does nullify a connection,
> while an aspect does not cut an aspect [but] hands over the sought thing, and a uniting cuts an
> aspect." — Intro Ch. 3, 44 (`:124`)

fn. 68: *"(1) 'uniting' or a conjunction by degree, (2) a connection by degree from another sign,
and (3) an aspect by sign. In other words, while degree-based connections can cut each other as in
type #1, aspects by sign do not cut each other."* ¶45–48 (Fig. 15) apply it: the Moon 10° Taurus
uniting with Mars 20° Taurus, connecting with Venus 15° Cancer first — *"the effect will be
ascribed to the planet being united to."* fn. 72: this is none of the three types exactly.

## 5. ★ Reception (49–57, Fig. 16) and non-reception (58–62, Figs. 17–18)

> "**49** And as for [8] reception, truly the reception of the planets is when a planet is
> connected with it from its exaltation or its house: for then it has perfect reception, with
> truthful intention. **50** And below this reception is if it is connecting with a planet from the
> triplicity of that planet being connected with. **51** And what is contrary to this, the
> astrologer has nothing to do with … **52** And an example of that is if the Moon is in Aries and
> she is connecting with Mars: then he receives her because [Aries] is his house; or she connects
> with the Sun, because he is the lord of the exaltation." — Intro Ch. 3, 49–52 (`:158`)

> "**54** And as for the reception of triplicity and bound, it is if the Moon in a question is in the
> bound of Venus, and [the Moon] is connecting with her, and Venus is the lord of the triplicity of
> the Moon as well as the lord of her bound … **55** And when the Moon is like that, then she is
> received: and this is the statement of Māshā'allāh on the reception of triplicity and bound."
> — Intro Ch. 3, 54–55 (`:164–172`)

**One direction only; house/exaltation perfect; triplicity below; bound only with triplicity
(Māshā'allāh's); the face never appears; a connection is always required.** Abū Ma'shar's fn. 207
confirms the attribution. Two further forms:

> "**56** And if the Moon was connecting with a planet and that planet was connecting with the lord
> of the house of the Moon or its exaltation, then the Moon is received." (fn. 75: *"like a transfer
> of light which indirectly allows for reception"*) **57** "And if the Moon was empty in course, and
> then she passed over into the next sign and connected with the lord of her first sign (or its
> exaltation), it is just like reception; and if she connected with a planet other than the lord of
> her first sign or its exaltation, it undermines her." (fn. 77: a version of *escape*, cf.
> *Aphorisms* #16–17) — Intro Ch. 3, 56–57 (`:174–176`)

> "**58** … if the Moon or the lord of the Ascendant connected with a planet, [and] that planet did
> not have testimony in the position of the Moon or the lord of the Ascendant, it will not recognize
> it nor receive it. **59** And likewise if the Moon or lord of the Ascendant connected with a
> planet from [the other planet's] fall, it would be like one who comes to it from the house of its
> enemies … **60** … the Moon is in Aries and she is connecting with Saturn, or in Cancer and she is
> connecting with Mars, or in Virgo and she is connecting with Venus, or in Capricorn and she is
> connecting with Jupiter, or in Libra and she is connecting with the Sun. … **61** Now if the
> indicator was in its fall, and it is connecting with a planet [and] that planet did not have a
> share in the position of the indicator (that is, by house or exaltation), it will not see it as fit
> for anything, as though the one asking is offering defeat … **62** And if a star connected with a
> planet in its own fall, or [in] the fall of the planet handing over to it, it brings it down." —
> Intro Ch. 3, 58–62 (`:178–204`)

**Figure 18 is in the OCR as a table** (`:206–212`): the five kinds, A→B: (58) B alien in A's sign;
(59–60) A in B's fall; (61) A in its own fall, B alien; (62) B in its own fall; (62) B in A's fall.
fn. 80: Pisces–Mercury is missing from ¶60 (present in the Latin). ⚠ fn. 73: ¶51 speaks of the
*astrologer* rejecting, where the planets should. This is the source of `NON_RECEPTION` Kinds I–V
and of *Questions* Ch. 1, 40–42.

## 6. Emptiness of course and the banished planet (63–64, Figs. 19–20)

> "**63** … the emptiness of the course, that is when the Moon is not connecting with any of the
> planets, and not uniting with one … **64** And the banished planet is the planet which none of
> the planets connects to." — Intro Ch. 3, 63–64 (`:228–230`)

fn. 85: *"In Abū Ma'shar's Gr. Intr. this is sharpened so that she does not complete a connection
with any planet while in her current sign."* fn. 86: "banished" is an early *wildness*, and
Figure 20 is drawn to the **later** definition (aversion to all), not Sahl's words. ⚠ Sahl's ¶64 is
about connections; `app.py` keeps it separate from Abū Ma'shar's whole-sign wildness for that reason.

## 7. Returning (65–69, Figs. 21–22)

> "**65** … returning, it is if a planet or the Moon is connecting with a retrograde planet or one
> under the rays: for it returns to it what it accepted from it, and has already corrupted its
> management, and indicates that the question does not have a beginning nor end. **66** And another
> manner of returning is that the light planet (and it is the one handing over) is in a stake, and
> connecting with a planet falling away from the Ascendant: so that sought thing has a beginning
> because the one handing over … is in a stake, but it does not have an end because the accepting
> one … is falling." — Intro Ch. 3, 65–66 (`:240–258`)

fn. 88: the manuscripts misplace the example; fn. 89: ⚠ "falling away from the Ascendant" (aversion)
vs "falling from the stake" — *"in 68, the planet returning the management is both."* Abū Ma'shar's
VII.5, 104–116 is the elaborated tree, and his ¶116 quotes Sahl's *"neither a beginning nor end"*
(VII.5 fn. 193).

## 8. The handings-over (70–76, Fig. 23)

> "**70** … handing over power, it is if a planet is connecting with a planet from its own house,
> exaltation, or triplicity. … **73** … handing over management and nature, it is if a planet is
> connecting with a planet from its house or from its exaltation: for it hands over its management
> and its nature to it. **74** And an example of that is if the Moon or any of the planets is in Aries
> and is connecting with Mars, or it is in Gemini and is connecting with Mercury (that is, in
> reception). **75** And if the Moon was in Taurus or Cancer, she hands over power and management.
> **76** And in signs other than these two, she only hands over management." — Intro Ch. 3, 70–76
> (`:268–280`)

fn. 90: *"In 76, Sahl says that any application or connection hands over management … A related step
would be connecting to her own lord, or management and nature—but this is the same as reception
(74)."* Three grades of one thing; ¶74's *"(that is, in reception)"* is Sahl's own parenthesis.

## 9. ★ Strength — eleven testimonies (77–89)

> "**78** Of them, the first is that a planet is in an excellent place from the Ascendant: that is,
> in the stakes or what follows them, of the places which look at the Ascendant. **79** The second is
> that a planet is in something of its own share … house or its exaltation, triplicity, bound, face,
> or joy. **80** The third is that it is direct in course. **81** The fourth is that there is not an
> infortune with it in its sign, connecting with it, or looking at it from a square or opposition.
> **82** The fifth is that it is not connecting with a star that is falling away from the Ascendant,
> or with a star in its fall, or [that] it is itself in its own fall. **83** The sixth is that it is
> advancing. **84** The seventh is that a masculine planet (and they are Saturn, Jupiter, <and
> Mars>) is eastern, arising at dawn. **85** The eighth is that the planets are in their own glow:
> that is, a masculine planet in the day, and a feminine planet in the night. **86** The ninth is
> that a planet is in fixed signs. **87** The tenth is that the planets are in the heart of the Sun
> (that is, when they are with him in one degree) … **88** The eleventh is that, of the quarters of
> the circle, the masculine ones are in the masculine quarters of the Ascendant (and they are from
> the Midheaven to the Ascendant, and from the fourth to the seventh), and the feminine planets are
> in a feminine quarter … and the masculine planets are in masculine signs, and the feminine planets
> in feminine signs." — Intro Ch. 3, 78–88 (`:294–332`)

Footnotes that matter: fn. 92 — six good places (the ninth excluded as falling); fn. 95 — advancing
is *"dynamically angular or succedent, i.e. by primary motion with respect to the angular axes, and
not by whole sign"*; fn. 96 — *"Mars is the questionable one here"*; fn. 97 — ⚠ ¶85 *"is an error,
and Sahl should have said a diurnal planet in the day, and a nocturnal planet in the night."* ¶87's
**one degree** for the heart is the corpus's other value beside Abū Ma'shar's 16′ (VII.2, 7).

## 10. ★ Weakness — ten ways (90–101), and Figure 24

> "**91** … if a planet was falling from the stakes and not looking at the Ascendant: and that is in
> the sixth and the twelfth. **92** … retrograde. **93** … under the rays of the Sun. **94** …
> connecting with the infortunes from an assembly, opposition, or square. **95** … enclosed between
> two infortunes, and that is if it is separating from [one] infortune and connecting with [another]
> infortune. **96** … in its fall. **97** … connecting with a planet falling away from the
> Ascendant, and it is separating from a planet receiving it. **98** … in a house in which it did
> not have testimony (neither house nor exaltation nor triplicity). **99** And, if a planet was
> western, the Sun having already overtaken it … **99** The ninth is if a planet was with the Head or
> Tail, if it does not have latitude. **100** The tenth is if they were inverted … in the seventh
> from their own house, and that is called 'unhealthiness.'" — Intro Ch. 3, 91–100 (`:338–364`)

fn. 99 doubts ¶97's two conditions must co-occur; fn. 103 on ¶99 (the Node) — *"in all likelihood
only the Moon's Node is meant"*. **Figure 24** (`:390–409`, in the OCR as a table) pairs strengths
and weaknesses; fn. 109: *"italics show my own suggested contrasts where Sahl has not listed
them"* — six of the weakness-column entries and three of the strength-column ones are **Dykes's,
not Sahl's**.

## 11. ★ The conditions of the Moon (102–118)

> "**103** … if the Moon is burned, within 12° of the Sun, not going past him, and likewise after
> him. **104** … in the degrees of her fall in Scorpio, or she is connecting with a star in its fall.
> **105** … opposed to the Sun, within 12° of him [but] not having reached the opposition. **106** …
> assembled with an infortune or looking at it from a square or opposition, or she is enclosed
> between two infortunes … **107** … with the Head or Tail in [one] sign, there being less than 12°
> between them. **108** … in the twelfth sign from her own house (and that is Gemini), or the last
> degrees of the signs (because they are the bounds of the infortunes). **109** … falling from the
> stakes or connecting with a planet falling from the stakes. **110** … in the burned path, and that
> is at the end of Libra and the beginning of Scorpio. **111** … wild: and that is empty of course,
> not connecting with any of the planets. **112** … slow in course (and that is when the equation
> is subtracted from it), or she is decreasing in light (and that is [at] the end of the [lunar]
> month)." — Intro Ch. 3, 103–112 (`:419–461`)

fn. 120: the burned path *"between 19° Libra (the fall of the Sun) and 3° Scorpio (the fall of the
Moon)"* per Abū Ma'shar (= VII.6, 40). fn. 122: ⚠ "wild" here = empty of course, not the later
aversion sense. Ten items; Abū Ma'shar's eleven (VII.6, 63–74) overlap only partly
(`app.py:5964` lists the differences correctly).

> "**115** For indeed if the Moon was increasing in her glow, and Mars was with her or looked at her
> from the fourth or seventh, it does violence to the Moon: because if the Moon was increasing in
> light … so that she is hot, Saturn will not harm her because he is cold, while Mars will harm her
> because he is hot. **116** And if she was decreasing in light … so that she is cold, Mars will not
> harm her, while Saturn will harm her because he is cold." — Intro Ch. 3, 115–116 (`:465`)

**= Abū Ma'shar VII.4, 86–89 exactly.** ¶117–118 extend it: Saturn *"in nativities of the day … at
the beginning of the month, and in the masculine signs, decreases harm"*; Mars the reverse. Not in
the engine.

## 12. Fortunate, unfortunate, enclosed (119–123, Fig. 25)

> "**119** And neither a planet, the Moon, nor a sign is called 'unfortunate' until an infortune is
> with it, or looks at it from the fourth, seventh, or tenth. **120** And a planet is not called
> 'fortunate' until the fortunes are in the stakes of that planet, or the planet is in the stakes of
> the Ascendant." — Intro Ch. 3, 119–120 (`:489`)

> "**121** … an enclosed [planet], that is if a planet is between the two infortunes, separating from
> one of them [and] connecting with the other, without another planet casting its rays between the
> two; and more powerful than that, and more unfortunate, is if the separation and the connection is
> at 7° and what is below that." — Intro Ch. 3, 121 (`:495`)

Example ¶122–123: Mars 10° Cancer, Saturn 18° Aries, Moon 13° Libra. Sahl's enclosure is
**connection-based** (separating/connecting) with a 7° grade; Abū Ma'shar's VII.6, 56–58 adds the
same-sign body/ray type and the 2nd/12th-sign type.

## 13. Glow, testimony, and the four joys (124–132, Figs. 26–29)

> "**124** … a planet in its own glow, it is if Mars was shining bright by night: [he is] in his own
> glow because he is nocturnal; and Saturn by day is in his own glow because he is diurnal." —
> Intro Ch. 3, 124 (`:511`)

fn. 132: for Mars this *"is equivalent to halb, at least in nocturnal charts"*; fn. 133: this sentence
and ¶125 track *Nativities* 1.23, 17–18 (Māshā'allāh). ⚠ So Ch. 3 has glow as **sect** here (124)
and as **gender** at 85 (fn. 97 calls 85 the error).

> "**128** … Mercury rejoices in the Ascendant, the Moon rejoices in the third, Venus rejoices in the
> fifth, Mars rejoices in the sixth, the Sun rejoices in the ninth, Jupiter rejoices in the eleventh,
> and Saturn rejoices in the twelfth. **129** … Saturn rejoices in Aquarius … Jupiter rejoices in
> Sagittarius, Mars rejoices in Scorpio, the Sun rejoices in Leo, Venus rejoices in Taurus, Mercury
> rejoices in Virgo, and the Moon rejoices in Cancer. **130** … the planets of the day rejoice when
> they are in the east (and that is when they are arising at dawn); and the planets of the night
> rejoice by night (and that is when they are arising in the evening from the horizon of the west).
> **131** … Saturn and Jupiter and Mars rejoice when they are in a masculine region … and the Moon
> and Venus rejoice when they are in a western, feminine region … **132** And Mercury rejoices in
> both regions together." — Intro Ch. 3, 128–132 (`:517–565`)

`JOY_HOUSES` = ¶128; the sign joys = VII.6, 19; the third joy (130) is the sect-correct form of ¶84
(fn. 138); the fourth (131) restates ¶88 with **Mars among the masculine three**.

## 16. Not synthesized

The three horary worked examples are transcribed only as data; the philosophy of ¶7–8 ("two men
under a single blanket", the vertical thread of fn. 53) is noted, not developed.
